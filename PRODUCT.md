# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

Middle school, college, and university students, plus hobbyist makers. No CAD experience assumed. They use NexCAD on school iPads (touch-first, Safari) or personal MacBooks (desktop browsers) to design enclosures for their STEM electronics projects. Their context: building something physical — a robot, a sensor box, a smart car — and needing a case to protect and mount the electronics before 3D printing.

## Product Purpose

NexCAD lets students and makers turn a set of existing electronic parts into a 3D-printable enclosure in minutes. The parts-first workflow means they pick real components (Arduino, sensors, motors) from a library, position them, and the system generates the enclosure — screw posts, standoffs, port cutouts, and all — automatically. Success is measured by the time from opening the app to having a downloadable STL file, not by how much the user learns about CAD along the way.

## Positioning

Parts-first enclosure CAD — design your case from the components up, not from a blank canvas.

## Brand Personality

Precise, approachable, efficient. A precision instrument that feels welcoming, not intimidating. The "Light precision instrument" theme carries this: cool slate background, clean floating panels with hairline borders, single blue accent, monospace numerals for dimensions. Professional confidence without professional complexity.

## Anti-references

- **Not toy-like**: avoids childish colors, oversized controls, or gamified interactions. Users are making real things for real projects.
- **Not cold/technical**: avoids command-line aesthetics, raw numeric UIs, or interfaces that read like engineering data sheets. The tool is for students, not industrial engineers.

## Design Principles

- **Parts-first**: every enclosure starts from real electronic parts, not primitive geometry. The component drives the case.
- **One accent, one job**: the blue accent marks exactly one thing — the primary action or selected state. Nothing competes for attention.
- **Precision feels light**: hairline borders, monospace numerals, tight but readable spacing. Accuracy is visible without feeling heavy.
- **Undo is free**: every action is reversible in one step. Users experiment fearlessly.
- **Works on iPad**: touch-first responsive layout, no right-click-only interactions, no hover-dependent workflows. Buttons and controls are sized for fingers.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA baseline. Color contrast ≥4.5:1 for body text, ≥3:1 for large text. Keyboard-navigable across all panels and the toolbar. Labels are bilingual (Traditional Chinese / English). The 3D viewport supports wireframe and x-ray modes as alternatives to the default solid-surface rendering.
